Results note, autonomous session, 2026-10-26

aweSOM on Apple Metal, and a retrain of the plasma-turbulence fiducial map

The statistically combined ensemble (SCE) step runs on the MacBook GPU through jax-metal and reproduces the CPU result to float32 precision. Retraining the 2,097,152-cell plasma-turbulence data set with the online and the new batch trainer gives maps of the same quality as the published fiducial map, but the merged cluster set at the paper's threshold is not stable from seed to seed.

Summary

Metal SCE

Works out of the box with jax-metal 0.1.1 (jax 0.4.34). On the full 28-map ensemble the Gsum table agrees with the NumPy run to a maximum relative difference of 5.5 × 10−7 with identical ranking; masks differ by at most 1.0 × 10−4. See section 3.

Paper fiducial reproduced

The shipped pickle, re-clustered with a merge threshold of 0.25 as in Appendix A of Ha et al. (2024), gives the paper's seven clusters with volume fractions 5.4, 1.7, 1.1, 2.6, 3.3, 1.5 and 84.5 %. See section 1.

Retrains

Online trainer: 31 s, quantization error 0.3257 versus 0.3239 for the fiducial. Batch trainer (40 epochs, CPU JAX): 156 s, quantization error 0.3050, topographic error 0.0098. Both recover the background at 85.0 %, but merge into 4 and 5 clusters instead of 7. See section 2.

Batch trainer on Metal

Not possible: jax-metal has no float64 and no jax.shard_map. The trainer now raises a clear ImportError under that JAX instead of an AttributeError. See section 5.

Uncertainty. Timings in Table 6 marked "contended" were measured while another training job was running on the same CPU cores; the idle-machine row is the one to quote. Seed-to-seed cluster counts in Table 4 come from one run per seed, so the spread is indicative only.

1. The fiducial result in the paper, and what the shipped pickle contains

The README links the method paper, Ha et al. (2024), arXiv:2410.01878. Its fiducial self-organizing map for the plasma-turbulence snapshot has 64 × 37 nodes, learning rate α0 = 0.1, N = 1283 training steps (one pass over every cell), uniform random initialization and a final neighbourhood radius of 8 (Section 3.1 and Appendix A). The U-matrix is partitioned into seven clusters by merging centroids whose boundary cost is below 0.25 of the cost between the two most distant centroids (Appendix A). Section 4.1 reports the volume-filling fractions as 5.4, 1.7, 1.1, 2.5, 3.3, 1.5 and 84.5 % for clusters 0 to 6, describes clusters 0 and 4 as isolated single current sheets, 1 and 2 as components of double current sheets, 6 as background, and gives a sheet-like total of about 11.5 %. The SCE over 36 realizations is thresholded at ΣGsum of 0.25, 0.5 and 0.8 (Section 4.2 and Figure 3). No quantization or topographic error is reported.

The repository ships that fiducial map as examples/plasma-turbulence/som_object.fiducial-5000-64-37-0.1-2097152-1u.pkl with the four features j_par, j_asym, b_perp, e_par, standardized to zero mean and unit variance. Re-clustering its lattice with assign_cluster_to_lattice(smoothing=None, merge_cost=0.25) reproduces the paper's result exactly, including the cluster numbering (Table 1). Three details did not match the text:

  • Stored clusters differ from the paper. The clusters array inside the pickle has seven clusters with fractions 6.5, 1.7, 1.1, 1.5, 69.8, 17.9 and 1.5 %, which no threshold among 0.2, 0.25 and 0.3 reproduces. It was evidently produced with a different setting (or smoothing) than the published figure.
  • The example script uses 0.2. examples/plasma-turbulence/run_plasma_som.py hard-codes merge_threshold = 0.2, which yields nine clusters on this lattice, not the seven in the paper.
  • Initial weight range. Appendix B says initial weights are drawn uniformly in −1 ≤ w0 ≤ 1; Lattice draws them in [0, 1), and the first history snapshot of the fiducial run confirms [0, 1). The paper also notes that all initializations converge to similar maps at this N, so this is a documentation discrepancy rather than a result-changing one.

2. Retraining the same data

Both retrains used the pickle's own standardized data_array and the fiducial hyper-parameters: 64 × 37 nodes, α0 = 0.1, 2,097,152 steps, uniform initialization, seed 42. The online run is Lattice.train_lattice(history=False) with the compiled step kernel from this branch. The batch run is Lattice.train_lattice_batch(epochs=40), the JAX batch map added on this branch, executed on the CPU JAX backend (jax 0.12) with one device. Lattices were then clustered at 0.25 and every cell labelled with Lattice.predict.

Quantization error (QE) is the mean Euclidean distance from each cell to its best-matching node over all 2,097,152 cells; topographic error (TE) is the fraction of cells whose first and second best nodes are not lattice neighbours. Jaccard is computed on the union of non-background cells, treating the largest cluster of each map as background. Precision and recall of non-background cells against the fiducial are 0.60 / 0.58 (online) and 0.54 / 0.52 (batch).

All three maps describe the data equally well: the online retrain lands within 0.6 % of the fiducial quantization error and the batch trainer is 6 % lower, with a slightly higher topographic error (the batch map is a touch less smooth). The background fraction comes out at 85.0 % in every case. What differs is how the U-matrix partitions into clusters at a fixed threshold of 0.25: the paper's seven-way split separates positive and negative j_par sheets, the two signs of j_asym, and both signs of e_par, whereas the online retrain merges the current-sheet families into one cluster per sign of j_par and keeps only positive e_par, and the batch map keeps negative e_par and three current-sheet clusters (Table 3). About 41 % (online) and 36 % (batch) of the non-background volume is shared with the fiducial. For scale, the 28 maps of the shipped ensemble (different sizes, learning rates and step counts) have between 5 and 9 clusters each.

The batch seed-43 run at 40 epochs merged into six clusters of which the largest holds only 47 % of the cells: the background was split in two, so "background" as the largest cluster is not meaningful for that run.

Across seeds 42 to 46 the online trainer gives 4 to 6 clusters with background between 83 and 89 %; the batch trainer gives 5 to 7. Quantization error is stable to the third decimal within each trainer. So the number of merged clusters at a fixed 0.25 threshold is seed-dependent for both trainers, and the paper's seven clusters is one draw from that distribution, which is the reason the paper runs the SCE over 36 realizations rather than reading a single map. Eighty batch epochs lowered the quantization error only from 0.2991 to 0.2969 and doubled the time, so 40 epochs is a reasonable default for this data set.

3. SCE on the MacBook GPU through Metal

aweSOM.sce picks JAX when jax.default_backend() is gpu or METAL, otherwise NumPy. A separate virtual environment .venv-metal was built with Homebrew Python 3.12, jax-metal 0.1.1 and the matching jax/jaxlib 0.4.34, numpy 2.1.3 and numba 0.68, with aweSOM installed editable from the research checkout. JAX reports Metal device set to: Apple M1 Max and default_backend() == "METAL", and the SCE log confirms the JAX path. The command in both environments was:

python -m aweSOM.sce --folder . --dims 128 128 128

Two inputs were used: a six-map subset (the first six maps in sorted order) and the full 28-map ensemble (193 clusters in total) stored as labels.*.npy in examples/plasma-turbulence/som_results.

The work scales with the number of cluster pairs, 193 × 192 / 2 ≈ 18,500 pairwise mask comparisons on a 1283 grid for the full ensemble, which is why the small subset does not amortize the Metal compile and transfer cost.

Two tests in tests/sce_test.py failed under the Metal environment and were test bugs, now fixed on the branch: test_jax_cpu_available patched the backend name but never re-imported the module, so it only passed on machines whose real backend was already CPU; test_compute_SQ asserted a Python float and a NumPy array where JAX returns device arrays. Both now reload the module under the patch and compare by value and shape.

5. Limitations and open points

  • Batch trainer cannot run on Metal. jax-metal 0.1.1 pins jax 0.4.34, which lacks jax.enable_x64, jax.shard_map and float64 support; the trainer is written for jax 0.12 with float64 accumulation. aweSOM.batch now raises an ImportError that says so (commit 926a9c0). Parallel GPU training therefore still needs a CUDA or ROCm machine, as noted in the session report.
  • Online trainer is CPU only by design. It is sequential and runs in numba; Metal plays no part in it.
  • Only the number-of-clusters question was swept. No attempt was made to tune the merge threshold per map; the paper's SCE over many realizations is the intended remedy, and that step is what Metal now accelerates.
  • Metal float32. Gsum and masks are float32 on Metal. The differences measured here are six orders of magnitude below the thresholds in use, but a future SCE criterion that compares Gsum values close to a threshold should be aware of it.
  • Timing caveats. The "contended" rows in Table 6 overlapped other jobs; the idle row was measured sequentially with nothing else running.
Nothing in the main checkout was modified. The pickle, the example scripts and the shipped SCE results in /Users/tvh0021/git_repos/aweSOM were only read. The documentation discrepancies in section 1 (stored clusters, threshold 0.2 in the example, initial weight range) are reported, not fixed, because each would change published example output.
